# Indian Wind Corridor O&M Standard Operating Procedures

## Chapter 1: Seasonal Monsoon Readiness & Moisture Mitigation

### Section 1.1: Pre-Monsoon Inspection and Desiccant Breather Replacement
<!-- chunk_id: CHK-SOP-001 -->
Monsoon wind farm pre-check desiccant breather replace: In Indian wind corridors (such as Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Rajasthan), the Southwest and Northeast monsoon seasons bring extreme relative humidity ($>90\%$) and severe cyclonic precipitation. Standard operating procedures mandate comprehensive pre-monsoon checks across all turbines 30 days prior to monsoon onset. Maintenance technicians must inspect and replace desiccant breathers on all gearbox housings, hydraulic power units, and main step-up transformers. Saturated silica gel indicating moisture absorption (color transition from cobalt blue to pink) must be replaced immediately with active orange/blue desiccant to prevent water vapor condensation inside high-speed drive cavities.

### Section 1.2: Substation Pre-Monsoon Electrical Safety & Grounding
<!-- chunk_id: CHK-SOP-002 -->
Monsoon wind farm pre-check and substation transformer oil sampling safety checklist: Prior to high-wind monsoon generation periods, wind farm substations must undergo thorough electrical protection testing. Maintenance crews must inspect 33kV/66kV switchgear, lightning surge arresters, and earth pit resistance. Earth pit electrode resistance must measure strictly below $1.0\,\Omega$. In conjunction with monsoon pre-checks, technicians must execute the substation transformer oil sampling safety checklist to verify dielectric breakdown voltage (BDV $\ge 50\,\text{kV}$) and ensure that moisture content remains below $20\,\text{ppm}$ to prevent catastrophic flashover during intense monsoon storm activity.

## Chapter 2: High Ambient Heatwave Protocols

### Section 2.1: Extreme Ambient Heatwave Operating Guidelines (Above 38°C)
<!-- chunk_id: CHK-SOP-003 -->
Ambient heatwave operating protocol above 38 celsius: During summer months in desert and semi-arid corridors (e.g. Jaisalmer, Barmer, Kutch), ambient temperatures regularly exceed $38.0^\circ\text{C}$ and peak above $45.0^\circ\text{C}$. The heatwave operating protocol dictates that thermal elevations in gearbox oil, generator windings, and converter semiconductors must be contextualized against ambient temperature. When ambient temperature reaches $38.0^\circ\text{C}$ or higher, all nacelle exhaust louvers must be locked fully open and auxiliary cooling blowers switched to continuous run mode. The operational context engine suppresses false mechanical alarms when component temperature rises are within nominal expected thermal offsets.

### Section 2.2: Heatwave Electrical Cooling and Thermal Derating Management
<!-- chunk_id: CHK-SOP-004 -->
Ambient heatwave operating protocol above 38 celsius and converter cooling: When ambient heatwave conditions persist above $38.0^\circ\text{C}$, transformer and frequency converter liquid cooling radiators experience reduced heat rejection capability. O&M teams must monitor coolant flow rates and verify water-glycol mixture concentration. If stator winding temperature approaches $140.0^\circ\text{C}$ during ambient heatwaves, the turbine control system initiates proportional active power derating ($100-200\,\text{kW}$ decrements) to maintain safe internal temperatures without tripping the unit, maximizing deemed generation availability during high-demand summer peak grid hours.

## Chapter 3: Pooling Substation Transformer Maintenance

### Section 3.1: Transformer Oil Sampling Protocol and Safety Checklist
<!-- chunk_id: CHK-SOP-005 -->
Substation transformer oil sampling safety checklist: Conducting oil sampling on 33kV/66kV/132kV main step-up transformers requires strict adherence to high-voltage substation safety protocols. Technicians must wear arc-flash protective equipment (NFPA 70E Level 4), verify grounding on sampling canisters, and obtain formal permit-to-work (PTW) clearance before approaching live transformer bays. Oil sampling for Dissolved Gas Analysis (DGA) and dielectric breakdown testing must be collected from the lower drain valve using airtight glass syringes, purging at least 2 liters of oil before collecting the analytical sample to ensure representative fluid extraction.
